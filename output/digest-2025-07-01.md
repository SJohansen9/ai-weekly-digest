### 📦 “Package hallucinations” create supply-chain attack vector
*Source: USENIX | 17 June 2025*

| Stakeholder | Impact |
|---|---|
| **Business** | Dev teams using AI coding assistants face real malware risk. CI/CD pipelines need package-existence checks. AI coding-tool vendors face pressure to add detection. |
| **Society** | Open-source ecosystem integrity at stake — typosquatting meets AI predictability. Developers who trust AI without verification are most exposed. |
| **Government** | Cyber agencies likely to issue advisories. Intersects with EU Cyber Resilience Act and software supply-chain regulation. |

**Why it matters:** This is one of the first documented cases where AI unreliability creates a predictable, exploitable attack surface. Because ~45% of hallucinated package names reappear on identical prompts, attackers can pre-register those names on npm or PyPI and wait for developers to install them.

**Likely responses:** DevOps teams block unknown packages by default. Package registries tighten namespace controls. AI vendors add package-verification guardrails.

### ⚖️ US Senate removes state AI regulation moratorium (99–1)
*Source: AP News | 1 July 2025*

| Stakeholder | Impact |
|---|---|
| **Business** | Win for firms needing clarity across states — no 10-year freeze. Tech companies lose push for federal preemption. Patchwork compliance continues. |
| **Society** | States retain power to protect children, artists, and workers. No federal floor — protections vary by state. |
| **Government** | States keep authority; federal AI legislation still absent. Continued state-by-state experimentation. |

**Why it matters:** The 99–1 margin shows bipartisan consensus favours keeping state authority rather than granting tech companies a decade of regulatory immunity. Its removal means the US enters a period of accelerating state-level AI law with no federal harmonisation in sight. For businesses, this is the worst compliance scenario short of a ban: rules that differ by jurisdiction and keep changing.

**Likely responses:** Big Tech continues lobbying for federal framework. Enterprises accelerate state-level compliance mapping. Civil-society groups celebrate but call for actual federal protections.
